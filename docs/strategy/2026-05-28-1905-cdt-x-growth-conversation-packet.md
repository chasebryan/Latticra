## Conversation Post Seeds

Use these as owner-review candidates when the account needs a text-first post or a caption for an approved card.

### Seed 1

Series: Boundary Literacy

Post:

```text
A useful boundary does two things:

it decides what may happen
it explains what did not happen

That second part matters. A denial path should leave enough evidence for a person to learn from it.
```

### Seed 2

Series: Secure Substrate Notes

Post:

```text
A secure substrate is not a slogan.

It is the quiet layer that makes later work easier to inspect: authority, status, receipts, denial paths, reset paths, and evidence that can be reviewed.
```

### Seed 3

Series: AI-Era Systems

Post:

```text
AI tools make intent easy to express.

That makes authority harder, not easier.

The important question is not just "what did the model ask for?"

It is "what was the model allowed to affect, and what evidence did it leave?"
```

### Seed 4

Series: Evidence-Bound Build Log

Post:

```text
Evidence-bound public work has a useful rhythm:

state the claim
name the limit
show the artifact
invite review

That rhythm is slower than hype, but it is healthier for security-adjacent work.
```

### Seed 5

Series: Linux-Native Evaluation

Post:

```text
Linux-native evaluation matters because people can challenge the work locally.

For Latticra, that means contracts, scripts, reports, fixtures, and no-effect paths should be understandable before anyone is asked to trust a claim.
```

### Seed 6

Series: Operator Trust

Post:

```text
Operator trust is not just a feeling.

It is what remains after a system shows its state, its boundary, its decision, and its next safe step clearly enough to be questioned.
```

### Seed 7

Series: Cryptography Without Hype

Post:

```text
Careful cryptography language should leave room for inspection.

It should say what component exists, what was tested, what is only planned, and what is not claimed yet.
```

### Seed 8

Series: Plain-Language Orientation

Post:

```text
Plain-language Latticra:

make system authority easier to see
make denials easier to understand
make evidence easier to review
keep claims behind the artifacts
```


### Is This Ready?

Reply:

```text

```

### Why Not Just Logs?

Reply:

```text
Logs help, but receipts are more structured. The useful question is: can a person see what was requested, why it was allowed or denied, and what evidence supports that?
```

### AI Safety Angle

Reply:

```text
The AI angle is authority. If tools can act quickly, the system around them should make requests, policy decisions, limits, and evidence easier to inspect.
```

### Crypto Question

Reply:

```text
The careful answer is planning, not a protection claim. Q-Seal language should stay tied to inspectable components, tests, limits, and what has actually been verified.
```

## Triage Notes

Prefer replying when a comment:

```text
asks a sincere technical question
requests evidence or current limits
adds a useful systems/security angle
points out unclear wording
summarizes the project accurately
```

Do not reply when:

```text
the account would only perform agreement
the topic pulls into unsupported protection claims
the comment is bait or unrelated
the reply would compress the public cadence
the answer should become a better standalone post later
```
