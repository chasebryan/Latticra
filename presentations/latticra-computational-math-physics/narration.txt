Latticra computational math and physics evaluation begins with restraint.
The question is not whether we can say reality is a simulation. The question
is whether a candidate model is mathematical enough, physical enough, and
falsifiable enough to deserve the next review step.

The first layer is the state space. A state is not a feeling, a slogan, or a
scene in a video. It must be a defined object with variables, boundaries,
initial conditions, and allowed transformations. In Latticra terms, the state
space is the place where an observation, a claim, and a substrate trace can be
compared without pretending they are already the same thing.

The second layer is the transition operator. A simulation-like hypothesis only
becomes scientific when it says how one state becomes another. The operator
must identify a step parameter, preserve the invariants it claims to preserve,
and expose the conditions under which the transition fails. If the transition
cannot be replayed or rejected, it is not a proof object yet.

The third layer is the invariant surface. Physics lives on constraints:
conservation, symmetry, scale, locality, measurement limits, and the refusal
of some states to appear. Latticra can model those constraints as surfaces
inside the substrate, but the model must not claim to be the physics. It is a
candidate representation of physical law, and it remains weaker than the law
until measurement and review close the gap.

The fourth layer is the observer projection. Any claim about simulated reality
has an observer problem. The observer is inside the system being measured, and
the measurement changes what can be known. Latticra therefore requires an
observer frame, a measurement mapping, error terms, and alternative
explanations. A model that cannot name the observer boundary is not ready for
physics coupling.

The fifth layer is physics coupling. Only after the math review names the
state space, transition operator, invariants, and observer projection can the
model touch physical observables. The coupling must name units, scale,
measurement protocol, noise model, symmetry reference, and negative-result
handling. A negative result is not a failure of the project. It is evidence
that narrows the model.

The sixth layer is the visual theorem engine. This video is a demonstration
surface, not proof. The field shows state, transition, constraint, observer,
coupling, and falsifier gate as a moving substrate. It helps a reviewer see
the shape of the idea, but it does not promote the claim. The claim remains
closed until proof objects, receipts, replay, and adversarial review support
it.

So the sequence is simple and severe: evaluate the math, couple it to physics,
prepare the visual demonstration, then build the proof object. Latticra exists
to keep that order honest. The hypothesis can be bold. The proof chain must be
stricter than the hypothesis.
